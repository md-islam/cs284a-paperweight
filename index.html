Title, Summary and Team Members

Title: Paperweight: Pop-Up Butterfly

Summary: Build an interactive paper butterfly that unfolds as a book opens. We will investigate how paper mass and stiffness affect the shape of the butterfly and the unfolding.

Team Members: Kate Chier, Sofia Vargas, Lauren Tang, Md Islam

02

Problem Description

Our project aims to extend the results of the paper “A Geometric Study of V-style Pop-ups” by adding considerations for different types of paper. The discussion section of the paper identifies the consideration of paper properties, such as thickness, weight, and warping, as a potential direction for future work with significant practical value. Accounting for these variations would make the model more versatile and applicable to real-world uses, particularly in areas such as video game graphics and animation. We plan to solve this problem by conducting preliminary research on the physical properties of different types of paper, adding different coefficients to the base equations accordingly, and running simulations of each paper type. Ultimately, we hope that this project will give us a stronger understanding of how physical materials affect the geometry and rendering of pop up books.

03

Goals and Deliverables

We plan to deliver an animation of a pop-up book, going from a closed to a fully-open state and vice versa, to display all the intermediate parts of the paper (un)folding. This can be included in our webpage as a GIF or embedded video. We can measure the quality of the created pop-up book by seeing if it folds and unfolds as planned in the animation, and also whether or not the folding behavior for at least 2 different paper materials is as expected.

Questions we aim to answer:

  • How does the folding behavior differ for the same pop-up book when using different materials?
  • How can we factor material types into our physics/rendering code?

We hope to deliver animations involving more than 2 distinct paper materials. We also are interested in the concept of motion fitting, where in order to produce a specific movement, we need to figure out the paper folds which would produce it – if we have additional time, we hope to implement this as well.

04

Schedule

Week 1:

Review geometric and physical pop-up simulation literature to clarify the technical contributions and understand how we might approach the unfolding simulation. Build physical butterflies and begin coding the pop-up built in code.

Week 2:

Implement flexible wings, gravity, and attachment constraints. Measure paper samples and calibrate parameters to add to the system. Test the system with default parameters on a butterfly.

Week 3:

Test additional parameters on the butterfly and collect videos of the animation and error measurements.

Week 4:

Finish comparisons and prepare the final paper, video, and presentation.
