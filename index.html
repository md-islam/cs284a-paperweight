Designing the Motion
of a Pop-Up Butterfly

Material-Aware Simulation of V-Style Pop-Ups

A study in paper, geometry, and motion.

We will build an interactive simulation of a paper butterfly that unfolds as a book opens. Building on geometric models of V-style pop-up mechanisms, we will investigate how paper mass and bending stiffness affect the butterfly’s shape and unfolding motion. We will compare simulated behavior across different paper materials and evaluate the results against physical prototypes.

02

Problem description

Paper mechanisms connect geometry to tangible motion. Our project aims to extend the ideas presented in A Geometric Study of V-Style Pop-Ups by incorporating physical properties of paper into a simplified folding simulation.

The referenced research studies geometric constraints for pop-up structures and identifies physical paper properties, including thickness, weight, and warping, as a direction for future work. In practice, different papers bend and deform differently under gravity, affecting both the appearance and motion of a pop-up structure.

We will investigate these differences for a butterfly attached to a pop-up book, combining a geometric folding mechanism with a simplified deformation model for its wings. The mechanism will prescribe the folding motion and attachment locations; a separate deformation model will approximate gravity and bending behavior. We do not plan a full finite-element paper simulator.

Technical challenges

  • Modeling the V-style folding mechanism.
  • Maintaining valid attachment constraints.
  • Representing wing deformation.
  • Accounting for gravity and bending stiffness.
  • Comparing simulated and physical behavior.
03

Goals and deliverables

Start with a constrained mechanism, then study how material behavior changes its motion.

Planned deliverables

BASELINE PLAN

  1. A 3D animation of a pop-up book opening from closed to fully open.
  2. A paper butterfly that unfolds with the book.
  3. A simplified geometric model of a V-style pop-up mechanism.
  4. A flexible wing representation with simplified gravity and bending behavior.
  5. Simulations using at least two distinct paper material configurations.
  6. Visual comparisons between materials.
  7. Basic validation using physical butterfly prototypes and measurements at selected opening angles.

Aspirational deliverables

IF TIME PERMITS

These extensions are exploratory goals, not guaranteed features.

  1. More than two paper materials.
  2. Interactive control of paper mass and stiffness.
  3. Quantitative parameter sensitivity analysis.
  4. Automatic estimation of effective material parameters from observed physical motion.
  5. Simplified motion fitting: optimizing selected fold parameters to approximate a desired butterfly movement.

Research questions

  • How does folding behavior differ when the same pop-up mechanism uses different paper materials?
  • How do mass and bending stiffness influence wing deformation?
  • How accurately can a simplified model approximate the behavior of physical paper?
  • Can model parameters be estimated from observed physical motion?

Evaluation plan

At fixed book-opening angles, we will compare wing-tip position and displacement, wing curvature or deformation, and visual differences across material configurations. Physical prototypes will provide reference observations; where measurements allow, we may report error between simulation and physical results.

The baseline focuses on one mechanism and two material configurations to keep implementation and validation manageable. All measurements and comparisons are proposed; no experimental results are available yet.

04

Four-week work plan

A sequence from geometric foundations to physical comparison and final communication.

WEEK 1

Geometry & prototypes

  • Review geometric and physical pop-up simulation literature.
  • Build physical butterfly prototypes.
  • Begin implementing the geometric V-fold mechanism.
  • Establish the simulation environment.

WEEK 2

Material behavior

  • Implement flexible wings, gravity, and attachment constraints.
  • Add material-dependent bending parameters.
  • Test the baseline simulator.
  • Prepare the graded milestone submission.

WEEK 3

Comparison & validation

  • Test additional material parameters.
  • Collect physical reference photographs.
  • Compare simulations with physical examples.
  • Collect error measurements and evaluate deformation.

WEEK 4

Evaluation & communication

  • Complete material comparisons and evaluation.
  • Refine visualizations and animations.
  • Write the final paper.
  • Prepare the final video and presentation.
