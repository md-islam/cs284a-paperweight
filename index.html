Paperweight: Pop-Up Butterfly

Material-Aware Simulation of V-Style Pop-Ups

A study in paper, geometry, and motion.

We will build an interactive paper butterfly that unfolds as a book opens. We will investigate how paper mass and stiffness affect the butterfly’s shape and unfolding motion.

Kate Chier@kxchierSofia Vargas@sofiamvLauren Tang@ltang16Md Islam@md-islam

UC Berkeley CS 184/284A · Fall 2026

02

Problem description

Our project aims to extend the results of A Geometric Study of V-Style Pop-Ups by adding considerations for different types of paper. The paper’s discussion identifies properties such as thickness, weight, and warping as potential directions for future work. Accounting for these variations would make the model more versatile and applicable to real-world graphics and animation.

We plan to investigate the physical properties of different paper types and incorporate material-dependent parameters into a simplified folding and deformation simulation. We will compare simulations across different paper materials to better understand how physical properties influence pop-up geometry, deformation, and animation.

A geometric V-fold mechanism will control the butterfly’s basic folding motion, while a separate, simplified flexible-wing model will represent deformation, gravity, and stiffness. Adjusting the rigid folding geometry alone does not model physical material deformation.

The technical challenges are maintaining valid attachment constraints as the mechanism moves, representing wing deformation, and choosing material parameters that produce plausible behavior.

03

Goals and deliverables

Start with a constrained mechanism, then study how material behavior changes its motion.

Planned deliverables

BASELINE PLAN

  1. A 3D animation of a pop-up book opening from closed to fully open and closing again.
  2. A paper butterfly unfolding and folding with the book.
  3. A simplified V-style folding mechanism.
  4. Flexible butterfly wings responding to gravity and material stiffness.
  5. Simulated folding behavior for at least two distinct paper material configurations.
  6. Visual comparisons of folding behavior.
  7. Evaluation of whether the butterfly behaves as expected for the selected materials.
  8. An animation suitable for embedding in the final project website as a video or GIF.

Aspirational deliverables

IF TIME PERMITS

These extensions are optional and are not baseline commitments.

  1. More than two paper material configurations.
  2. Additional quantitative analysis of material parameters.
  3. Optional motion fitting: given a desired butterfly movement, estimate fold parameters that approximate the target movement.

Research questions

  • How does the same pop-up mechanism behave when constructed from different paper materials?
  • How can paper properties be represented in the simulation?
  • How do material properties affect the butterfly’s shape and movement?

Evaluation plan

We will observe unfolding and folding behavior, compare material configurations, and inspect wing deformation. We will compare wing positions at selected book-opening angles and use available measurements or observations to evaluate simulation quality.

All evaluation is proposed; no measured results or numerical accuracy targets have been established.

04

Four-week work plan

A sequence from geometric foundations to physical comparison and final communication.

WEEK 1

Geometry & prototypes

  • Review geometric and physical pop-up simulation literature to clarify technical contributions and understand the unfolding simulation.
  • Construct physical butterfly prototypes.
  • Begin implementing the pop-up mechanism.

WEEK 2

Material behavior

  • Implement flexible wings, gravity, and attachment constraints.
  • Measure paper samples and calibrate the material parameters.
  • Test the simulation using default parameters on the butterfly.

WEEK 3

Comparison & validation

  • Test additional material parameters.
  • Collect animation videos.
  • Measure and evaluate simulation errors where reference measurements are available.

WEEK 4

Evaluation & communication

  • Complete material comparisons.
  • Finalize the paper, video, and presentation.

Resources

Research references

  1. A Geometric Study of V-Style Pop-Ups: Theories and Algorithms

    Xian-Ying Li, Tao Ju, Yan Gu, and Shi-Min Hu · 2011

    Reference for V-fold geometry and its opening and closing motion.

  2. Generating Animated Paper Pop-Ups from the Motion of Articulated Characters

    Conrado Ruiz Jr., Sang N. Le, and Kok-Lim Low · 2015

    Reference for generating animated pop-up mechanisms from desired motion, particularly the optional motion-fitting extension. Our planned wing deformation model is a separate implementation component.

Proposed computing platform

We plan to develop on ordinary development computers in Visual Studio Code using JavaScript and Three.js. Starting from an existing Three.js scene, we will extend it with butterfly geometry, folding animation, and material simulation. The simulator has not yet been implemented.

The exact source link for the starting scene still needs to be supplied before submission.
